# Research and return

Checked 2026-09-05 against the cited primary pages and the current Build 41 continuation. This supports design implications only; it does not establish habit formation, retention, health benefit, or the efficacy of any Room of Days mechanic.

## Supported implications

- **Choose a small field, while preserving choice.** Stable-context repetition varies widely across people and behaviors ([Lally et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674); [Singh et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)), and specific if-then plans can support action initiation ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1)). This supports an optional cue and a deliberately small set of concrete actions. It does **not** validate three as an optimal number. “Up to three” is a product capacity choice.
- **Make completion and its consequence exact.** Expected tangible rewards can feel controlling in the experiments reviewed by [Deci et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/), while competence-supporting feedback is a plausible design target ([Sheeran et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32437175/)). The safe implication is to name the completed action and actual XP/stat/currency result, keep undo available, and avoid moral or personality judgments. It is not evidence that the RPG economy causes useful behavior.
- **Keep the return neutral and actionable.** The self-compassion studies concern difficulty and health self-regulation, not this app ([Breines & Chen](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22645164/); [Biber & Ellis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28810473/)). They support testing a return that preserves prior work and offers a smaller next move without catch-up language. [Johnson et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6096297/) also confirms that gamification findings are mixed and bundled, so XP, streaks, and collectibles remain authored product choices to evaluate in context.

## Build 41 capabilities already preserved

- **Choosing up to three:** `GoalsPage._chooseToday` and `QuestsPage._chooseToday` use the same `showTopThreeWizard`; `applyDailyField` stores a date and deterministic rank, caps accepted titles at three, and leaves unchosen Quests open. `goals_choose_today_test.dart`, `day_planning_test.dart`, and `quests_daily_field_test.dart` cover persistence, order, commitments, snoozed selections, and optional work.
- **Clear completion and immediate reward:** selection and completion are separate actions; `_runCompletion` marks the exact Quest done immediately, plays one accepted cue, stages the XP flight/receipt, commits atomically after 520 ms, flushes on lifecycle changes, and keeps the completed card in place for undo. `quest_selection_flow_test.dart`, `journal_quest_entry_test.dart`, `widget_test.dart`, and `reward_motion_accessibility_test.dart` cover exact ownership, one reward, undo, and reduced-motion semantics.
- **Returning after absence:** a planned goal exposes “this doesn’t fit today,” then offers **Make it smaller**, **Prepare the return**, or **Leave today alone**. The first two revise the owned plan and return through Workshop acceptance before a replacement Quest reaches the board; leaving mutates nothing. Separately, the engine either spends enough freezes to hold the whole gap or starts a fresh streak with a comeback reward while preserving the best streak. `goals_quest_management_test.dart`, `simulation_test.dart`, `streak_freeze_test.dart`, and `streak_freeze_widget_test.dart` cover those invariants.
- **Domain continuity exists, but only partly:** totals, ranks, best streak, date-count history, domain notes, and goal notes persist. The newly reachable stat chips open `DomainDetailScreen`, whose “Recent gains” links completion titles to a domain.

## Top continuity defect

The automatic domain record is not long-term history. `GameState.commit` keeps only the newest **eight ledger entries across all domains** (`engine.dart`, lines 1858–1862); `LedgerEntry` stores no date; and `DomainDetailScreen` shows at most four matching entries (`domain_detail.dart`, lines 53–56). After enough unrelated completions, a domain’s concrete past actions disappear even though its aggregate score remains. Existing tests cover stat-chip reachability and save round-trips, but none assert domain-attributed completion history across time or beyond eight rewards.

The next continuity fix should be one schema-owned slice: preserve a bounded, date-stamped completion archive by domain, migrate the current ledger without losing entries, and add retention/round-trip plus domain-screen tests. Keep the present totals, notes, return choices, reward timing, and Build 41 visual language unchanged.
