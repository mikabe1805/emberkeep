## Waves — transformed tapping

[Stone Sound Design?](https://youtube.com/shorts/f5Ub8BuSr10?si=yHUakLxPZiI1cYth)

The literal stone collision is not the target. The useful pattern is a close
physical gesture transformed into a different world's event: concentrated
contact, 80–200 ms of shaped body, subtle pitch/motion processing, and a played
multi-hit cadence. The final section keeps a crisp onset while its overall
spectral center is lower than the raw/demo regions. The lesson is authored
translation, not material realism.

## David Dumais — fantasy layer architecture

[Resonance Stone sound-effect breakdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V7mAmppz26A)

The caption-backed breakdown assigns a separate job to ambience, contact,
kinetic body, low support, tonal identity, motion, and spatial treatment. EQ,
compression, Trackspacer, tail editing, tuning, and placement make those layers
behave as one event. For Room of Days, the lesson is not to put seven layers on
every tap; it is to make every remaining layer solve one perception problem and
share the same mix logic.

## Riot — event and world system

[So You Wanna Make Games?? | Episode 8: Sound Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KcorIwJscFA)

Riot frames sound as gameplay information, feedback, and emotion; explains how
frequency and envelope change meaning; and explicitly describes reverb as both
place and a gluing mechanism. The product translation is a semantic event
director plus one shared frequency, envelope, dynamic, and space grammar. The
audition unit must be a real interaction sequence, not a playlist of solo WAVs.

## Combined conclusion

The prior system failed because it varied the surface of each file without
defining one acoustic physics beneath them. The corrected order is:

1. one imagined cause;
2. one shared transient/body/pitch/space grammar;
3. semantic gestures inside that grammar;
4. context sequencing and mix priority;
5. only then, per-cue polish and runtime routing.
