# Working experience comparison

Final root confirmation: the release rebuild and offline preparation passed.
The rebuilt browser's modal tree exposes only session controls, running copy
matches the active timer, and cancellation restores the underlying Quest page.
Current narrow controls and wide-room captures are recorded in `design-qa.md`.
The earlier pending browser gate below is resolved by that current evidence.

Each sheet shows the approved exploratory concept on the left and the final
Flutter golden on the right. Both full images are fitted to a common height
with a neutral gutter. Nothing was redrawn, composition-cropped, or otherwise
semantically altered.

## Severity

All remaining differences are **P3 polish differences**. The final renders
now carry the room's fine wall grain through the translucent working surface,
and committed actions use the established textured brass material. I found no
P2 visual or interaction-hierarchy issue in these final sheets. The target is
a faithful, useful adaptation of the product's real model and routes, not a
pixel copy of exploratory mock-task art.

## Goals today — P3

The approved concept devotes more of its frame to a broad desk tableau. The
production render gives more of that space to the actual saved Top Three: two
selected choices, a capacity count, the route to choose today, and available
quests. Its fine-grained glass and brass primary action now read as the same
warm material system as the rest of the room.

The remaining difference is compositional: the production selected-quest area
is less like a physical still life than the concept. It is a P3 opportunity for
future spatial richness, not a loss of the day-planning job or a hardcoded
goal metaphor.

## Choose today — P3

The concept makes recommendations look like objects arranged across a desk.
The product presents the useful equivalent through a direct chooser: retained
selections remain visible, timed suggestions state their real reason, and
Browse all exposes ordinary available tasks. The adaptive single-session rail
now avoids a vacant structural slot.

The concept retains more bespoke task tokens and furniture-like depth. The
current rows are plainer, but their rank, filtering, exact identity, save, and
cancel behaviors are real. That difference is P3 material/detail polish.

## Reasoned choices — P3

The selected-one, ten-minute production state preserves the central trust
claim: a reason comes from a saved timer and an accepted current goal route.
The live filter and selected state are compact, and the adaptive rail plus
textured brass give this narrower state a finished material treatment.

The exploratory image has more illustrative differentiation among choices.
The production version has less iconographic drama, while showing a truthful,
actionable filtered state. This is P3 visual differentiation work only.

## Session workspace — P3

The production timer has a clear elapsed/remaining relationship, one focused
primary action, and the same warmed glass, fine grain, and brass as Goals. It
now reads as a physical working surface rather than a flat brown pane.

The concept gives more visual weight to a full desk narrative and secondary
music/note objects. Those are a P3 compositional distinction in the golden;
the browser evidence below verifies that the corresponding optional controls
and note behavior are real rather than invented decoration.

## Smaller attempt — P3

This remains the closest pairing. Both frames communicate that a hard day can
be adapted without discarding the original route. The product makes the real
review and acceptance operation, revised duration, rank, and retained note
legible in the working-room material language.

The concept uses a more expressive physical note treatment. The production
version is quieter and more audit-readable, leaving only P3 expression and
depth differences.
