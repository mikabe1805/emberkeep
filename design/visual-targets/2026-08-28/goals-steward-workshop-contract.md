## Product split

- The ordinary Goals destination remains the user's private, character-free
  Goal Study.
- The steward exists only in his own workshop.
- The workshop appears for goal creation, meaningful route planning or repair,
  and rare completion/archive work. It does not appear on ordinary returns or
  merely after an absence.
- Quests remains the place where the action is performed and completed.

Goals must answer three questions without a dashboard:

1. What is the person actually trying to change?
2. What is the one defensible next action?
3. How can the route change without erasing earlier proof?

## First production slice

The quick route composer collects the desired outcome, current reality,
visible proof, normal time budget, likely snag, and optional horizon. The
deterministic planner drafts the route. Submitting that route enters four
user-readable destinations: an intimate desk folio, a wide-room planning
waypoint, registered live lettering at the arch, then the steward's separate
tavern workshop. At the wide waypoint, the same exact first move and lighter option are
readable across the room and `Cross the room` owns the next camera leg. Each
advance moves through one continuous apartment only after an explicit action.
The arch copy stays typographically continuous as its clasp appears and
disappears; the tavern workshop resolves around that same action instead of
swapping in unrelated UI.

The final workshop beat shows:

- the Goal and current marker;
- the exact first Quest cut from the route;
- why that marker comes first;
- the visible proof it is meant to create;
- the remaining route kept in order;
- one primary action that explicitly takes the Quest;
- quiet controls to change the cut or return to the Goal Study.

The Quest does not join the board until the person takes it. Canceling keeps
the drafted Goal route resumable and creates no orphan or duplicate Quest.

## Interaction grammar

`real state change -> physical action -> one authored line -> exact choice`

The first slice uses the restored spatial room progression and one direct
workshop handoff at its arrival. It does not use typewriter dialogue, a greeting
loop, a dialogue tree, a relationship meter, daily NPC copy, or praise
inflation. Press feedback begins at contact. The camera uses distinct physical
legs: a quick withdrawal from the desk, an indefinite user-controlled wide-room
stop, a slower approach to the arch, a faster doorway crossing, and a quieter
workshop settle. The painted room can pick up brief, low-amplitude velocity
softness only while it is actually moving; all live type and controls stay
sharp, and every destination resolves completely crisp. Camera, motivated
light, action copy, workshop material, supporting facts, and controls settle at
deliberately different speeds; every effect is absent at rest. Reduced Motion
presents the same finished desk, wide waypoint, threshold, and workshop stills
in the same explicit-action order without camera travel or transition blur.

## Art and provenance boundary

The earlier no-runtime-character boundary is superseded by the owner's direct
request to place the steward we made into the workshop. The canonical anchor
and its identity lock remain the authority for every regeneration. The first
runtime tavern plate is
`assets/pages/goals-workshop-tavern-steward-v1.webp`, with its lossless source
preserved under `design/source-assets/runtime-originals/` and its complete
prompt and provenance recorded in `assets/pages/GENERATED-ASSETS.md`.

This authorization does not waive the consistency gate: face ratios, hair,
left earring, overshirt, apron, brass fasteners, sleeve patch, pocket placement,
and exactly two recognizable sharpened wooden pencils must survive every scene.
Runtime placement still needs rendered phone evidence and owner/device
acceptance before the character art is called final.

## Cadence after the slice

Later expansion may use the workshop for a genuinely changed aim, meaningful
route repair, and a rare completion/archive moment. Routine quick support,
ordinary returns, ordinary marker completions, and absence returns stay
character-free and fast. The first slice does not expand that cadence before
owner review.
